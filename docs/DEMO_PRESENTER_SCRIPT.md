# Presenter script: Sarah Lim / Brightpath (about 6–8 minutes)

**You** play **Daniel**, the operator. You sit at the laptop and drive the controls.
**Your friend** plays **Sarah Lim**, the caller.
Both of you speak into the same laptop mic.

## Controls you need

| Do this | How |
|---|---|
| Start the call | Dock (bottom right): **Start mic** |
| Pause to explain the screen | **Pause** (top right), or press **P**. Everything stays on screen and the mic stops. |
| Carry on | **Resume**, or press **P** |
| Finish | **End call** (top right). The wrap-up notes appear. |
| Ended by mistake | **← Back to call**, then **Resume** |
| Show how it works (technical audience) | Press **T**; press **T** again to hide |

## Rules for a smooth run

1. **Take turns.** Leave about one second of silence after each line before the other person speaks.
2. **Before pausing**, wait until the last line appears as a full bubble (not "speaking…"). Then press **P**.
3. **While on hold, talk freely.** The mic is off, so nothing you say goes into the transcript.
4. **After Resume**, wait for the green **On call** with the moving bars, then speak.
5. **Small slips are fine.** The system matches what you say to the script loosely. If you skip a line, just carry on.

## Before the audience arrives
- [ ] The dock dot is **green** (press **D** to see the details: Prepared 8/8, all checks green). Press **D** again to close the dock.
- [ ] Technical view is **off**: no small grey captions on the turns.
- [ ] Press **Reset**. The header reads **Ready for next call**.

## Opening (mic off, about 20 s)
> "This is what an advice-centre operator sees during a live call. It listens to both sides, tells the operator what to say next, and pulls up the caller's records automatically. My colleague is going to call in as Sarah."

Click **Start mic**. Wait for the green **On call**.

## Act 1: Who is calling?

| | Line |
|---|---|
| **Daniel** | Good afternoon, thank you for calling the Employment Advice Centre. My name is Daniel. May I have your full name, please? |
| **Sarah** | Hi Daniel, my name is Sarah Lim. |
| **Daniel** | Thank you, Ms Lim. Could I have your NRIC number so I can pull up your records? |
| **Sarah** | Sure, it's **S, eight eight two three four five one, D**. |

Wait for the Caller card to turn green (**Returning customer · 2 prior cases · 1 open**), then press **P**.

### ⏸ Pause 1: instant identity and records (about 45 s)
Point at the screen as you go:
- **Conversation:** "Every line is labelled Staff or Customer automatically, even though we share one microphone."
- **The amber moment:** "When Sarah gave her name, it found a *possible* match but deliberately didn't reveal anything. The suggestion told me to verify her NRIC first."
- **Caller card:** "The NRIC was captured the moment she said it. Her name and address were then filled in from the case system. She never said her address, and the ✓ shows it's verified."
- **Prior cases:** "She's a returning customer: two cases with the same employer, and one is still open."

Press **P** to resume. Wait for **On call**.

## Act 2: Why is she calling?

| | Line |
|---|---|
| **Daniel** | Thank you. Can I just confirm your address? |
| **Sarah** | It's 12 Tampines Street 45, #08-112, Singapore 520012. |
| **Daniel** | That matches our records. How can I help you today? |
| **Sarah** | It's about my employer again, Brightpath Logistics. My September salary came in four hundred and fifty dollars short. They called it an admin penalty, but nobody ever told me about any penalty. |
| **Daniel** | I'm sorry to hear that. Did you get a payslip or anything in writing explaining the deduction? |
| **Sarah** | The payslip just says admin penalty. And honestly, it started after I complained about my annual leave. My supervisor also cut my shifts from five days to three. |

Wait for the **Suggested response** showing **Based on: CASE-2026-03117 · Open**, then press **P**.

### ⏸ Pause 2: advice that knows the history (about 60 s, the key moment)
- **Suggested response:** "This is the moment that matters. The assistant connected today's salary deduction to her **open** leave complaint as possible retaliation. It tells me to add this to the existing case rather than open a duplicate, and gives me the words to say."
- **"Based on" chips and the highlighted case:** "It shows exactly which records the advice comes from. It can only cite cases that really exist."
- **Moments in the conversation:** "The story of the call is narrated as it happens: identity verified, returning customer, linked to the open case."
- *(Technical audience, optional)* Press **T**: "Each turn shows how the speaker was identified. The suggestion shows its source and timing." Press **T** again.

Press **P** to resume. Wait for **On call**.

## Act 3: Next steps

| | Line |
|---|---|
| **Daniel** | I can see you still have an open leave case with us against Brightpath. When did the shift cut start, and did anyone mention your complaint? |
| **Sarah** | About two weeks after I filed it, in early September. My supervisor told me people who make trouble don't get full shifts. I have it in a WhatsApp message. |
| **Daniel** | That message is really important. Please send us a screenshot and your last three payslips. I'll link this to your open leave case and set up the salary claim for mediation. |
| **Sarah** | Okay, thank you. Will it take as long as last time? |

Optionally, Daniel ad-libs a closing line ("We'll be in touch within a few days, Ms Lim."). Then click **End call**.

### ✎ Wrap-up (about 45 s)
- **Wrap-up notes:** "When the call ends, the notes are already drafted: the issue, a summary, the linked cases, next steps and the documents we asked for."
- Click **Copy to case notes**: "That's the after-call work done in one click. No retyping."

## Closing (about 15 s)
> "So in one call: the operator knew who the caller was without asking twice, got advice grounded in her real case history at the right moment, and finished with the notes written. Happy to take questions, or show how it works under the hood."

Press **Reset** before the next run.

## If something goes wrong

| What happens | What to do |
|---|---|
| A line gets the wrong label | Click the bubble to flip it. Keep going. |
| An error alert, or nothing is transcribed | **P**, then **D**. Choose **Autopilot**, then **Continue**. It plays the script from where you are. Use **Next →** or the → key to go line by line. |
| The suggestion is slow | Keep talking; it will catch up. |
| You pressed End call too early | **← Back to call**, then **Resume** |
| You want to start over | **Reset** |

## Likely questions

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| Is this real-time? | Yes. Transcription is live, and suggestions arrive within a couple of seconds of the customer speaking. Press **T** to show timings. |
| How does it tell the two speakers apart on one mic? | Voice diarisation, plus matching against the expected conversation in this demo. Staff can correct any line with one click. |
| Where does the customer data come from? | A read-only lookup on the case system, triggered as soon as the NRIC is heard. |
| Could it make things up? | It can only cite case IDs that exist in the record; anything else is discarded. Case details stay hidden until identity is verified. |
| Is the script needed in real life? | No. The script makes the demo repeatable. Real calls rely on diarisation, and everything else works the same. |
| Is any of it pre-computed? | For reliability, each scripted line also has a prepared suggestion. It shows only if the live one is slow, and the live answer replaces it. Press **T**: the chip says **Prepared** or **Live**. |
